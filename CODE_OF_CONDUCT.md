# Code of Conduct

neurOS and ORION are scientific and engineering projects. Participation should make the work more rigorous, more understandable, and safer to reuse.

## Expected behavior

Contributors, maintainers, reviewers, and users are expected to:

- discuss ideas, evidence, and code without personal attacks;
- distinguish disagreement with a method from judgment of a person;
- state uncertainty, limitations, conflicts of interest, and relevant provenance when they affect a technical claim;
- avoid overstating software, hardware, biological, clinical, or safety evidence;
- respect privacy and do not post identifiable neural, behavioral, health, credential, or security-sensitive data without authorization;
- give appropriate credit for upstream methods, datasets, software, and contributions;
- make review feedback actionable and technically specific;
- preserve an environment where researchers, engineers, students, clinicians, and device developers can participate without harassment or intimidation.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes harassment, threats, discriminatory or demeaning language, deliberate exposure of private information, plagiarism, falsification of results or provenance, knowingly misleading scientific claims, and publication of exploit details that create avoidable security or participant risk.

## Scientific disagreements

Strong technical disagreement is welcome. When a disagreement concerns a scientific claim, prefer a reproducible artifact, benchmark, counterexample, protocol critique, or proposed falsification test over rhetorical escalation.

Maintainers may ask that disputed claims be relabeled to the weakest evidence tier supported by the repository until stronger evidence exists.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove abusive content, lock discussions, reject contributions, or restrict participation when behavior materially harms people, project integrity, security, or scientific trust.

For conduct concerns that should not be public, contact the repository maintainer privately through GitHub. Security vulnerabilities should follow `SECURITY.md` rather than this process.

Enforcement decisions should be proportionate, documented when appropriate, and separable from the technical merits of a contribution.
